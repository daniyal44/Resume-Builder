Resume writing guide

How to write a resume in 2026 (step by step, with examples)

A resume has one job: convince a stranger, in about six seconds, that you are worth a conversation. This guide shows you the structure that works, the bullet formula that produces evidence, and the mistakes that quietly cost interviews.

1. Collect your raw material first

Writing at the template is why resumes sound generic. Before you open a builder, list your last three roles and for each one note:

  • the exact job title, employer, location and start/end dates;
  • three to five things you actually did, in plain language;
  • numbers attached to any of them — volume, revenue, users, time saved, team size, error rate;
  • the tools and methods you used, using the industry's real names.

This takes twenty minutes and makes everything afterwards mechanical. If you have an existing CV, you can import the PDF into OwnResume and edit the text instead of retyping it.

2. Use the standard structure

Reverse-chronological order remains the expected format in every major market. Recruiters read it fluently, and parsers handle it reliably.

  1. Name and contact block — email, phone, city, portfolio or LinkedIn. Plain body text.
  2. Headline or summary — one line naming the role, two or three lines of context if useful.
  3. Experience — most recent first, achievement bullets under each role.
  4. Projects — for career changers, juniors and technical roles.
  5. Education — degree, institution, year; move it above experience only if you graduated recently.
  6. Skills — a short, honest list of tools, languages and methods.
  7. Optional — certifications, languages, volunteering, publications. Cut anything that does not support the target role.

3. Write each section well

Contact block

One email you check, one phone number with country code, city and country, and one link you are proud of. Skip your full street address, date of birth, photo and marital status in the US, UK, Canada and Australia.

Summary or headline

Two or three lines maximum, specific to the role. Never a personality description.

Works Product manager with 6 years in B2B SaaS. Led two zero-to-one launches and grew activation from 34% to 61%. Looking for a senior PM role in developer tools.
Doesn't work Hardworking team player with excellent communication skills and a passion for technology.

Experience

For each role: job title on one line, employer and location on the next, dates right-aligned or on the same line. Then three to five bullets. Use present tense for the current role, past tense for previous ones.

Skills

Group logically — languages, frameworks, tools, methods — and keep it to what you would defend in an interview. A flat list of twenty tools you touched once reads as padding.

4. The bullet formula that produces evidence

Every bullet should be: action verb + what you did + measurable result. When you have no number, use scope instead — team size, budget, number of stakeholders, frequency.

Duty (weak) Responsible for social media accounts and reporting.
Achievement (strong) Rebuilt the paid social reporting workflow in Looker, cutting weekly reporting time from 6 hours to 40 minutes across 3 markets.

Useful verbs: led, launched, rebuilt, reduced, increased, automated, negotiated, migrated, mentored, shipped. Avoid “responsible for”, “helped with” and “assisted in” — they describe presence, not contribution.

Aim for one metric per bullet where possible, and never invent one. Approximations are fine when honest (“~2,000 users”).

5. Tailor for each job — but keep it honest

A tailored resume is not a rewritten resume. Change four things per application:

  • the headline or summary, so the target role is the first thing read;
  • the order of bullets inside each role, leading with the most relevant;
  • the skills list, so it mirrors the job ad's vocabulary;
  • which optional section appears — projects, certifications or languages.

That is a ten-minute edit with a big effect on both recruiter attention and automated screening.

6. Mistakes that quietly cost interviews

  • No numbers. Without scale, every claim sounds like every other candidate's.
  • Duties instead of outcomes. A job description tells what the role required, not what you achieved.
  • Inconsistent dates or titles. Gaps then look like errors rather than choices.
  • Walls of text. Bullets of 25 words or more are skimmed, not read. Keep them under two lines.
  • Unverifiable skills. “Expert in Python” with no project or role that shows it invites doubt.
  • Unexplained gaps. One short line — caregiving, study, travel — is enough.
  • Design over content. Skill bars, icons and photos squeeze out the evidence that matters.
  • No proofread. One typo in the first line undoes a strong page.

Final checklist before you send

  • Six-second test: does the top third say who you are and what you have achieved?
  • Every bullet starts with a verb and most carry a number.
  • The role you are applying for appears in the headline.
  • No line is longer than two lines on screen.
  • Dates are consistent and reverse-chronological.
  • Contact details are correct and clickable.
  • Exported as a text-based PDF, one or two pages, named Firstname-Lastname-Resume.pdf.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a resume be?

One page below roughly ten years of experience, two pages beyond. Recruiters scan, so length only helps when every line carries evidence.

What is the best resume format in 2026?

Reverse-chronological, single-column, with a short summary, achievement bullets and a plain skills list — the easiest format for both humans and parsers.

Do I need a resume summary?

It helps when changing careers, returning after a break, or targeting a specific role. Otherwise a one-line headline naming your role is enough.

Should I include a photo, age or address?

In the US, UK and Canada, no. City and country are sufficient. Some European and Asian markets expect a photo, so follow local norms.

Can I write my resume with AI?

Use it to restructure your own facts and tighten wording, never to invent achievements. Every claim must survive an interview.
