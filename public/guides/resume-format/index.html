Resume format guide: sections, order, length and layout

Format decides whether your content gets read. This guide covers which layout to choose, the order recruiters expect, how long your resume should be, and the typography settings that keep it legible on screen and in print.

The three resume formats

Format How it works Best for Risk
Reverse-chronological Roles newest first, each with achievement bullets Almost everyone, every industry Lowest — the default choice
Functional (skills-based) Skills grouped at the top, job history listed briefly Large career gaps, heavy career change Recruiters read it as hiding something
Combination / hybrid Short skills summary above a chronological history Career changers with transferable skills, technical roles Medium — can duplicate content

Use reverse-chronological unless you have a specific reason not to. It is what hiring managers expect and what parsers handle most reliably.

Section order that recruiters expect

  1. Name + contact details — top of page one, in plain text.
  2. Headline or summary — two or three lines, targeted to the role.
  3. Experience — most relevant and most recent first.
  4. Projects — optional; essential for juniors and career changers.
  5. Education — above experience only if you graduated in the last two years.
  6. Skills — grouped, honest, and matched to the job ad.
  7. Certifications, languages, volunteering — only when they support the application.
Rule of relevance The most persuasive evidence goes in the top third of page one. A recruiter may never reach section six.

How long should a resume be?

  • 0–5 years of experience: one page.
  • 5–10 years: one page if tight, two if genuinely necessary.
  • 10+ years or senior leadership: two pages, with the last ten to fifteen years detailed.
  • Academic CV: no fixed limit — publications, grants and teaching dictate length.

A second page is only justified when it carries additional evidence. If you are padding with a hobby list or unrelated coursework, cut back to one page.

Typography, margins and spacing

SettingRecommendedWhy
Body fontInter, Calibri, Arial, GeorgiaUniversal, prints cleanly, parses reliably
Body size10–12 ptBelow 10 pt is unreadable in print
Name18–24 ptImmediate identification
Section headings11–14 pt, uppercase or boldScanning anchors for humans and parsers
Line spacing1.15–1.4Airy enough to skim, compact enough to fit
Margins1.5–2.5 cmPrevents clipping and keeps text lines comfortable
ColourOne accent, near-black body textPhotocopy safety and contrast

Avoid two competing accent colours, all-caps paragraphs, and fonts below 10 pt. Those choices look designed on screen and fail on paper.

Format by situation

First job or internship

Education first, then projects, part-time work and skills. Lead with coursework and projects that show relevant ability rather than unrelated jobs.

Career change

Use a hybrid format: a short transferable-skills summary, then chronological history with bullets rewritten toward the target role.

Returning after a break

Keep chronological order, add one line explaining the gap, and lead with your most recent relevant work or retraining.

Senior or executive

Two pages, a leadership summary at the top, and bullets focused on scope, revenue and organisational impact rather than tasks.

Technical roles

Keep a clean skills block and a strong projects section with links. Show the stack you used per role rather than one undifferentiated list.

Academic CV

Sections in a fixed order: education, appointments, publications, grants, teaching, service. Length follows the record, not a page limit.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best resume format in 2026?

Reverse-chronological order in a single column. It matches recruiter expectations, survives applicant tracking systems and scales from a student resume to a senior CV.

Should a resume be one page or two?

One page below roughly ten years of experience; two pages beyond that, with the last ten to fifteen years detailed. Length should follow evidence.

What font and size should a resume use?

Inter, Calibri, Arial or Georgia at 10–12 pt body text, 18–24 pt name, 1.15–1.4 line spacing and 1.5–2.5 cm margins.

Does resume format matter for ATS?

Yes. Tables, text boxes, headers and multi-column layouts can scramble the parsed record. Single-column with conventional headings is safest.

What is the difference between a resume and a CV format?

A resume is a targeted one-to-two page summary. A CV is a comprehensive academic record of education, publications, grants and teaching, and can run many pages.
